Cosine Similarity
Cosine similarity is a method used to measure how similar two vectors are 
  by comparing the direction/pattern of their values, rather than their size

In our RAG system, we use it to compare the query's TF-IDF vector with the TF-IDF vector of each chunk.
and always we should use the main trained vectorizer that is trained on the vocabulary and values as TF_IDF so here 
it directly assign the value

#cosine comparison
A high cosine similarity score means the query and chunk have similar patterns of important words.
A low score means their patterns are different.
A score close to 1 means very similar direction.
A score close to 0 means little or no similarity.

Therefore, cosine similarity allows us to give each chunk a similarity score 
and retrieve the chunks with the highest scores as the most relevant results.

In short:
Query → TF-IDF vector → compare with chunk vectors using cosine similarity → similarity scores → retrieve highest-scoring chunks

### Small Example

Suppose our vocabulary is:
[cuda, python, gpu]

The query:
"cuda gpu"

becomes:

Query = [0.8, 0, 0.6]

A relevant chunk:

"cuda improves gpu performance"

becomes:

Chunk = [0.7, 0, 0.5]

The two vectors have a very similar pattern:

[high, 0, high]
[high, 0, high]

Therefore, their cosine similarity will be **high**, meaning the chunk is likely relevant to the query.

An unrelated chunk might be:
[0, 0.8, 0]

Its pattern is very different from the query, so its cosine similarity will be **low**.
**Idea:** Co  sine similarity checks whether the important values appear in similar positions in the two vectors.
